======================================================================
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
======================================================================

The app has evolved from a friendship map into a large personal ecosystem application containing:

- A friendship graph with people, groups, health nodes, and life areas
- Fancy and Simple map modes
- Stack / feed views
- Relationship scoring and history
- Health lists and editable historical records
- Health Connect data
- Photos and profile windows
- Calendar integration
- Undo/redo, lasso, vine drawing, tools, settings, and search
- Day/night modes
- Decorative vines, leaves, butterflies, fireflies, flowers, and group borders
- A diagnostic benchmark system
- Early work on adaptive performance modes
- Early work on person-to-profile camera transitions
- Many recent fixes to health-list behaviour and Fancy-map performance

The main performance lesson is:

DO NOT GUESS AT THE BOTTLENECK.

The built-in benchmark proved that the expensive systems were not always the visually obvious ones. The benchmark-and-measure workflow produced major gains:

- Group border rendering improved from roughly 4 FPS to around 33-43 FPS
- Ordinary vine leaves improved from roughly 12 FPS to around 59-73 FPS
- Vines improved from roughly 31-44 FPS to around 60-63 FPS
- Surrounding flowers improved from roughly 33 FPS to around 55 FPS
- Everything enabled improved from roughly 6.7 FPS to around 26.9 FPS

The largest successful architectural change was replacing thousands of repeated SVG objects and repeated raster generation with cached rasterised layers.

Later work on Living Forest adaptive quality and profile-growth transitions produced runtime errors or remained untested. Those features must be reviewed carefully against the actual live code before being trusted.

======================================================================
2. DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

1. Preserve 100% of existing functionality unless a deliberate UX change is agreed.
2. Do not rewrite the entire app at once.
3. Make one contained change at a time.
4. Build and test after every change.
5. Commit every successful change separately.
6. Benchmark before and after performance changes.
7. Avoid speculative architecture for graph sizes the app will never reach.
8. Do not introduce quadtrees, workers, or a custom renderer unless evidence shows they are needed.
9. Prefer exact root-cause fixes over broad optimisation passes.
10. Do not make architectural assumptions based only on snippets.
11. The app currently has no meaningful automated regression suite, so manual testing discipline is essential.
12. Any large structural split should be preceded by a small regression-test harness.

======================================================================
3. EARLY PERFORMANCE PLAN AND REPRIORITISATION
======================================================================

The original broad plan included:

- Lookup Maps instead of repeated nodes.find / links.find / filter calls
- Memoising vine geometry
- React.memo
- Component splitting
- Viewport culling
- Spatial indexing
- Web Workers
- A custom rendering engine
- Adaptive quality
- Undo-history redesign
- Codebase modularisation

After inspecting the actual code, priorities changed.

Confirmed problems included:

- Every vine performed two full nodes.find scans for its endpoints
- Every vine performed another links.some scan
- Vine geometry used expensive trigonometry and SVG construction
- A broad useMemo dependency array invalidated expensive work
- The giant App component meant unrelated state changes could trigger extensive work
- Pan/zoom state could trigger full-component rerenders

Deliberately deprioritised:

- Spatial indexing / quadtrees
- Web Workers
- A Figma-style custom rendering engine
- Large architecture work before fixing known inefficiencies

Reason: the intended map size is likely around 30-50 people, not thousands. Complexity should match actual scale.

The Simple map was smooth while the Fancy map was very laggy. This strongly indicated that core map interaction, basic pan/zoom, and node count were not the primary problem.

- Vines

- Butterflies / fireflies
- Under-node fading
- Surrounding flowers
- Group borders and border leaves
- Dark mask
- Filters and shadows

A diagnostics panel was added with independent toggles and an automated benchmark runner.

Timestamp: 2026-07-15T20:00:11.425Z
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Every render recreated an off-screen canvas for each group, redrew all border leaves, and PNG-encoded the canvas using toDataURL().

Cache each group’s rasterised border-leaf image. Regenerate only when group geometry or relevant appearance changes.

7. SUCCESSFUL HEALTH-LIST WORK
======================================================================

7.1 Stack health lists were unresponsive

Symptoms:
- Health lists could not be clicked in Stack view
- Map view worked correctly
- Stack view could appear to retain yesterday’s status

First attempted theory:
Pointer capture / drag timers. This did not solve it.

Actual root cause:
Health-list cards were visually rendered much lower than the Health section’s declared height. Following Stack sections overlapped them invisibly and captured taps.

Fix:
Correct the Health section height to include the health-list grid.

Result:
Health lists became interactive.

7.2 Stack health-list alignment

Problem:
Visible card position, connector anchor, drag grid, and section height used different vertical coordinate systems.

Fix:
Use one shared vertical origin for:
- Visible card
- Connection line
- Drag/drop
- Section height

Result:
Alignment became much better.

7.3 Larger buttons and configurable columns

Requested:
- Larger touch targets
- About six buttons per row
- Icons about 20% smaller

Refinement:
Each list should independently support 1-6 columns.

Implemented:
- Per-list column setting from 1 to 6
- Automatic wrapping
- 1-column, 2-column, 3-column, etc. cards can coexist
- Six-lane packing grid for Stack layout

7.4 Drag error

Error:
ReferenceError: healthListMembers is not defined

Cause:
Scope/name error in drag collision logic.

Fix:
Rebuild the relevant member collection inside drag scope.

7.5 Editable previous-day history

Feature:
- Lock/unlock toggle in history window
- Locked: display-only
- Unlocked: historical squares tappable
- Values cycle upward
- Completed value wraps to zero
- Numbers appear only while unlocked
- Closing automatically locks editing
- Uses existing habitHistory and habitToday persistence

User confirmed this worked very well.

7.6 Fancy-map health-list dragging

Bug:
Health cards called the drag handler with the entire node object, while the handler expected node.id.

Fix:
Pass node.id for health lists and health metric cards.

Status:
Implemented but not yet tested by the user.

8. STORAGE WARNING
======================================================================

Warning:
“Persistent storage denied by Android - data may be at risk of being cleared”

Cause:
navigator.storage.persist() is a browser API. Capacitor Android WebView may return false or not support it even while app-private storage works normally.

Fix direction:
- Skip browser persistence warning in native Capacitor builds
- Keep browser persistence request silent for browser builds
- Preserve localStorage, IndexedDB, Preferences, and photo/file backup systems

Important:
The warning was misleading, but the broader persistence system still deserves long-term review.

10. PROFILE-GROWTH TRANSITION
======================================================================

Design goal when tapping a person in Fancy map:

- Camera drifts towards them
- Person is framed around 40% down the screen
- Decorative animations temporarily freeze
- Portrait gets a highlight ring
- Ring expands toward the existing profile panel
- Existing profile panel cross-fades in
- Closing reverses the transition

Implementation advice:
Use a shared-element illusion:
- Temporary clone starts at the person’s screen position
- Final profile remains a fixed-position normal React panel
- Do not keep interactive text inside a scaled SVG

Status:
A first implementation was produced but not tested.

Risk:
This touches camera state, profile state, search, animation freezing, and gestures.

11. NAVIGATION DESIGN
======================================================================

Major concept:
The forest should feel like a place, not a canvas.

Proposed navigation:

- When zoomed into a neighbourhood, off-screen people/groups appear as directional edge buttons
- Each button appears on the edge in the direction of the destination
- Tapping smoothly pans/zooms to that destination
- Start with no more than one primary destination per edge
- Keep free dragging for exploration and editing

Navigation hierarchy:

1. Navigate to an area / neighbourhood
2. Explore local people/groups
3. Tap a person
4. Grow their profile from their portrait

Potential future features:

- Breadcrumb history
- Follow-the-path navigation along vines
- Glowing route vines
- Camera easing
- Destination pre-rendering
- Freeze off-screen areas during travel
- Reduce decoration during travel
- Restore quality near arrival
- Group framing based on bounding box
- Person framing at closer zoom
- Health-list destination framing

Recommended first navigation version:

- Directly connected people/groups only
- Up to four edge buttons
- Straight-line eased movement
- No complex path-following yet

- Buds opening
- New leaves

- More colour and movement

Nothing is dead. Some areas are simply waiting to flourish.

15. ECOSYSTEM IDEAS
======================================================================

15.1 Bees

Meaning:
Active care and flourishing relationships.

Behaviour:
- Attracted to open flowers
- Occasionally visit growing relationships
- Pass closed buds
- Travel between flourishing people in the same group
- Can arrive after a positive interaction
- Spend much of their time landed/resting to reduce render cost

Suggested states:
- Flying
- Landing
- Resting
- Cleaning
- Takeoff

Minimal data:
- x
- y
- angle
- targetFlower
- state
- timer

15.2 Butterflies

Meaning:
Joyful recent moments / relationship improvement.

Desired motion:
- Flutter
- Drift
- Glide
- Turn
- Avoid straight paths
- Rest on flowers sometimes

Performance issue:
Butterflies/fireflies remain one of the largest remaining SVG object counts.

Future options:
- Shared sprites
- Canvas layer
- Adaptive density
- Lower update rate

15.3 Fireflies

Meaning:
Night ambience and relationship signals.

Ideas:
- Keep coloured fireflies because their status meaning is useful
- Synchronised blinking clusters
- Background murmuration of low-detail dots
- Red fireflies can be targeted more by bats, but decorative predation must not hide the underlying relationship status

15.4 Bats

Night-only ambience.

Desired realistic low-cost movement:
- Glide with wings open
- Bank by tilting wings
- Occasional corrective flap
- Short rapid flap burst during insect chase
- Wings stop moving during glide

Minimal state:
- position
- velocity
- heading
- wing phase
- bank angle

Possible interaction:
- Occasionally intercept decorative fireflies
- Prefer red decorative fireflies more often
- Fireflies replenish
- Relationship warning remains stable through another indicator

Rendering recommendation:
- Low-detail silhouettes/shadows
- Canvas or sprite animation
- Avoid detailed multi-element SVG bats

15.5 Owl

Rare night event:
- Large shadow
- Silent glide
- No detailed wing animation needed
- Perhaps once every 5-10 minutes
- Briefly darkens the map beneath it

15.6 Day birds / swifts

Why swifts:
- Small silhouette
- Low detail
- Fast movement
- Mostly in flight

Motion:
- Glide
- Bank
- Small flap
- Fast curved turn
- Exit screen

Only one or two visible at once.

15.7 Pollen

Day ambience:
- Low-detail particles
- Shared wind vector
- Subtle
- Should be felt more than consciously noticed

15.8 Background firefly murmuration

Night ambience:
- Many tiny low-detail dots
- Coordinated drift
- Shared field / sine-wave movement
- No collisions or independent physics
- Best drawn on Canvas

15.9 Wind

Shared system:
- windAngle
- windStrength

Used by:
- Pollen
- Leaves
- Butterflies
- Bees
- Bat glides
- Firefly cloud
- Other ambient particles

23. IMPORTANT LESSONS FOR THE NEXT DEVELOPER / AI
======================================================================

1. Benchmark data is more trustworthy than visual intuition.
2. Reducing SVG count alone may not help if expensive canvas encoding still runs.
3. Cache keys must include only values that affect output.
4. Do not include pan/zoom transform in geometry memo dependencies unless geometry truly depends on it.
5. Never call toDataURL() repeatedly during ordinary renders.
6. Avoid creating off-screen canvases inside hot render paths.
7. A huge React component increases initialization-order and scope errors.
8. Functional state callbacks were implicated in minified runtime errors; use named testable updates.
9. Any adaptive loop must be isolated and cleaned up carefully.
10. The app’s future strength is the emotional model of cultivating a living forest.
