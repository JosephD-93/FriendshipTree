# FriendshipTree Project Bible

**Documentation version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Canonical living document  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-22

## Mission

FriendshipTree exists to help people build, maintain and understand meaningful relationships over the course of their lives.

It must support human connection rather than replace it. Every feature should reduce friction around remembering, reconnecting, caring and following through.

## Vision

Most digital products optimise for attention. FriendshipTree should optimise for connection.

The long-term goal is a calm, private and useful personal relationship platform that helps users understand their social world, remember what matters and take thoughtful action.

## Core principles

### People first

Technology is secondary. Real relationships are the purpose.

### Encourage, never shame

The app must not punish users for inactivity or frame relationships as failures. It should offer gentle, practical encouragement.

### Flourishing instead of failing

Relationships may be flourishing, healthy, growing, quiet or dormant. They should not be presented as dead, disposable or morally failed.

### Calm before complexity

The system may contain rich information, but the interface should reveal only what is useful at the moment.

### Organic, meaningful design

The living forest, vines, leaves, flowers, insects and mascots should communicate information naturally. Decoration must not obscure function.

### Information without overwhelm

The user should be able to explore deeply without facing an overloaded screen.

### Privacy and ownership

The user's relationship data belongs to the user. Export, backup, restoration and migration must be understandable and dependable.

### Offline resilience

Core use should remain available without a network connection wherever practical.

### Performance is part of design

Beauty is not successful when it makes the app frustrating or unstable. Visual richness must be measured against speed, battery use and reliability.

## Experience goals

FriendshipTree should feel:

- warm;
- calm;
- organic;
- supportive;
- curious;
- personal;
- trustworthy.

It should not feel like:

- a business CRM;
- a surveillance system;
- a guilt machine;
- an attention trap;
- a social-media feed.

## The living forest

The living forest is a representation of the user's social world, not a decorative skin.

Visual elements should communicate state carefully:

- flourishing relationships can support richer leaves, flowers and life;
- quieter relationships can remain simple and calm;
- reduced activity must not look like decay or punishment;
- animation should clarify or delight without becoming noise.

## The mole family

The mole characters represent different areas of life while remaining part of one recognisable family.

Current direction:

- Social Mole may use a bow tie;
- Health Mole may use a cap;
- Knowledge Mole may use glasses;
- all retain a pink nose;
- emotion is primarily shown through eyes, eyebrows, posture and movement;
- visible mouths and teeth should generally be avoided.

Mascots should encourage action without becoming demanding notification characters.

## Data philosophy

The application must work toward:

- reliable local persistence;
- visible backup status;
- exports that include both structured data and photos;
- restoration that can be tested;
- backups that survive uninstall when the user expects them to;
- clear ownership and portability.

No storage change should be considered complete until closing and reopening the Android app has been tested.

## AI philosophy

AI may reduce friction, organise information, summarise, suggest and help the user notice useful patterns.

AI must not become the emotional centre of the product. Human relationships remain primary.

AI output should be explainable, optional and respectful of private data.

## Development philosophy

Prefer:

- exact current files over reconstructed files;
- small testable changes over broad rewrites;
- evidence over assumptions;
- one maintained architecture over parallel replacements;
- reversible changes over destructive migration;
- documented decisions over chat-only memory.

Every substantial feature should answer:

1. Does it support meaningful relationships?
2. Does it make the experience calmer or clearer?
3. Does it respect user data?
4. Can it be maintained and tested?
5. Does it preserve acceptable performance?

## Long-term direction

Potential areas include:

- social graph and relationship groups;
- calendar and reminders;
- health and habit tracking;
- Android home-screen widgets;
- contact onboarding;
- secure backups and restoration;
- cross-device synchronisation;
- helpful AI assistance;
- FriendshipTree Studio;
- Forge update management;
- developer and community extensions.

Items in this list are vision, not confirmation of implementation.

## FriendshipTree Constitution

These principles should not be compromised casually:

1. Relationships are not disposable.
2. The product encourages rather than shames.
3. User data remains exportable and recoverable.
4. No manipulative engagement mechanics.
5. Beauty must not defeat usability or performance.
6. New architecture should simplify rather than fragment.
7. Current files must be inspected before being replaced.
8. Important work must be testable and reversible.
9. Offline and local resilience should be preserved wherever practical.
10. People remain at the centre.

## Definition of success

FriendshipTree succeeds when users spend less time managing relationships and more time enjoying them.

The technology should quietly support the person, then move into the background.
